## The starting point

The Pelican Hotel is a Ghaziabad hotel competing for people who are already nearby and already deciding —
someone searching "hotel near me" from a car, or a family comparing three options on a phone. For that
customer the Google Business Profile *is* the website. They will look at photos, glance at the rating,
and tap **Directions**.

## Why direction requests are the number that matters here

For a hotel, a direction request is the closest thing Google gives you to intent. A website click can be
idle curiosity. A direction tap means someone has opened navigation to your front door.

**1,389 of the 1,836 recorded actions were direction requests** — 76% of everything that happened on the
profile. Call taps came second at 360. Website clicks were a distant 77, and chat barely registered at 10.

That distribution is worth reading carefully, because it says something the hotel can act on: almost
nobody is going to the website from Google. The profile is doing essentially all of the work. Investment
belongs in the photos, the rating and the profile completeness — not in a website redesign.

## What we did

Nothing exotic. The profile was completed properly — category, hours, amenities, a real photo set rather
than two exterior shots — and then maintained: regular photos, and replies to reviews.

The rating sits at **3.5 from 209 reviews**. That is the honest constraint on this profile, and it is the
single biggest lever left. At 3.5 a hotel is filtered out by anyone using Google's "4.0+" toggle. Moving
that number is a service problem as much as a marketing one, and we have said so to the client.

## What this does not prove

We were given six months of profile screenshots and nothing before them. So there is no percentage
growth here, and we are not claiming any. There is no booking log, no revenue figure, and no way to tell
how many of those 1,389 direction requests became guests. What the evidence supports is precisely this:
in six months, the profile recorded 1,836 actions, and three quarters of them were people asking for the
way there.
